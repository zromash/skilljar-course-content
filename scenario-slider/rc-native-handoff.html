Step through what happens after you hit Send on a Native Message.

Scenario: You want to wish a recruit good luck before their game tonight using a Native Message so it comes from your personal number. Walk through what happens from Send to delivery.

Step 1 of 6
Set the message type to Native Message and preview it.
The preview catches errors — like missing merge fields — before you send.
Step 2 of 6
Click Send.
You get confirmation the message was created — but it hasn't reached the recruit's phone yet.
Step 3 of 6
Status shows "Sending."
C+R is preparing the handoff to your device — this isn't the same as a message actually going out over SMS.
Step 4 of 6
Status updates to "Delivered Notification."
This means you've been notified — not the recruit. Apple doesn't let any third party send iMessages on your behalf.
Step 5 of 6
A new task appears in My Tasks.
The task tells you one message is ready to send from the mobile app — this is the step that actually reaches the recruit.
Step 6 of 6
Open the task in the mobile app and send it from your device.
Only now does the message leave as a real iMessage. If it's no longer relevant, you can expire it instead so it drops off your task list.

✓ You now know why Native Message always ends with a task — it's the one channel Apple requires you to send from your own device.
